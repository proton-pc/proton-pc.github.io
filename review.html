Privacy and trust: what the company documents

This review of Proton VPN starts where the service puts its weight: privacy. Proton AG, based near Geneva, runs it under Swiss law. Its feature pages say it keeps no activity logs, that Swiss law cannot oblige it to start logging, and that Switzerland sits outside the EU and outside the large surveillance alliances. Those are the company's statements; what backs them is that every app is open source and that the company commissions independent audits and publishes the full results.

On the server side, the documentation lists bare metal servers with full disk encryption, the company's own DNS servers to stop DNS leaks, IPv6 leak protection and perfect forward secrecy, which creates a new key for each session. The protocols are WireGuard and OpenVPN. According to the protocol page, OpenVPN uses AES with 256 bit keys for data, 4096 bit RSA for the key exchange and HMAC SHA384 to check certificates.

The homepage also carries a comparison table, last updated in April 2026, that contrasts the service with ExpressVPN, CyberGhost, NordVPN and Surfshark on open source code, public audits and ownership. It is the vendor's own table, so treat it as a claim to check; the alternatives page lists those rivals with links to their own sites.

Features that matter day to day

The paid plans unlock most of the list below. The free plan keeps the kill switch, Stealth, DNS leak protection and the no logs policy.

S

Secure Core

Sends traffic through a first server in Switzerland, Iceland or Sweden, in hardened data centres, before it leaves the network. Paid plans.

N

NetShield

DNS filtering that blocks malware domains and, on VPN Plus and Unlimited, ads and trackers.

K

Kill switch

Blocks all traffic if the tunnel drops, per session or permanently. Every plan, the free one included.

T

Stealth

A custom protocol that carries WireGuard over TLS to get past VPN blocks. Every plan.

/

Split tunneling

Keeps chosen apps or IP addresses outside the tunnel on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, Android TV and the browser extension.

P

Port forwarding and Tor

Port forwarding for P2P, and Tor servers that open .onion sites in an ordinary browser.

The free plan in practice

The free plan is the strongest argument for the service. It has no data cap and no ads, and it keeps the kill switch and the Stealth protocol that paying users get. The cost is flexibility: one device at a time, a speed tier the plan table calls medium, and servers in 10 countries that the app picks for you, so you cannot choose where you appear. Streaming, P2P and NetShield are not included.

The pricing FAQ says the free tier has no artificial speed limits, while the plan table lists its speed as medium next to highest for the paid plans. Both statements sit on the same page; the fair reading is that free users are not throttled on purpose but share a smaller pool of servers. The free vs Plus comparison lines the two plans up row by row.

Speed claims, read carefully

This site did not run speed tests, so the numbers here are the company's. Paid plans use servers of up to 10 Gbps. VPN Accelerator, a set of the company's own techniques on all paid plans, is said to improve speed by up to 400% on long distance connections. The setup guide adds a sensible caveat: a VPN cannot make a slow home line faster, and the best levers are a nearer server and one with a lower load.

Quick Connect picks the fastest server for your location, and Smart Protocol probes the network to choose a protocol, which helps on networks that block VPN traffic. Alternative routing goes further and reaches the servers through third party networks such as AWS when direct access is blocked.

Price and value

VPN Plus costs $9.99 for a single month. The Black Friday offer running on 26 November 2026 cuts the first year to $35.88, or $2.99 a month, and the plan then renews at $83.88 every 12 months. The Unlimited bundle adds the paid tiers of the company's mail, storage, password and calendar apps for $77.88 in the first year, then $119.88. The full table is on the pricing page.

Against that, the free plan means you can judge the apps before spending anything, and the 30 day money back guarantee covers the first month of any paid plan. If the free tier already does what you need, the download page explains what you get and how to install it.

Frequently asked questions

Is Proton VPN safe to use?

The company documents a no logs policy under Swiss law, open source apps with published independent audits, full disk encrypted servers and its own DNS servers to prevent leaks. These are vendor statements rather than this site's tests, but the code is public for anyone to inspect.

Is the free plan really free?

Yes. It costs nothing, has no data cap and shows no ads. It is limited to one device, servers in 10 countries chosen at random and a medium speed tier, and it does not include streaming, P2P or NetShield.

Does it work with Netflix?

Streaming is a paid feature. The company says VPN Plus and the Unlimited plan unblock services such as Netflix, Amazon Prime Video and Disney+, while the free plan does not include streaming.

Does it allow torrenting?

Yes, on paid plans. P2P and BitTorrent are included in VPN Plus and Unlimited, with port forwarding to help P2P performance. The free plan does not include P2P.

Where is the company based?

Proton AG is registered near Geneva, Switzerland, and the service runs under Swiss privacy law. The company says Swiss law cannot force it to start logging what users do.
