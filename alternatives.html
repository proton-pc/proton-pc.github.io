The main Proton VPN alternatives are NordVPN, ExpressVPN, Surfshark, CyberGhost, Mullvad and Private Internet Access, the services the company names on its own site. Its comparison table, updated April 2026, says none of the four big rivals it lists has open source apps or a publicly available no logs audit. Those are the vendor's claims, so check each rival's own pages before switching.

What the company's comparison table says

Anyone weighing Proton VPN alternatives will meet the comparison table on its homepage. The summary below reproduces its rows. The footnotes say ExpressVPN's audit reports sit behind a non disclosure agreement, CyberGhost's Deloitte report has to be requested from Deloitte, and NordVPN and Surfshark show their audits only to logged in customers. The NordVPN entries for ad and malware blocking refer to its Basic plan.

Six alternatives in brief

This guide does not quote rival prices, because it has not read their pricing pages. Compare them on each official site on the day you buy.

  • NordVPN: owned by Nord Security, Panama in the table; ad and malware blocking need a plan above Basic.
  • ExpressVPN: owned by Kape, British Virgin Islands; audit reports behind a non disclosure agreement, per the table.
  • Surfshark: owned by Nord Security, Netherlands; audits visible after login, per the table.
  • CyberGhost: owned by Kape, Romania; a Deloitte no logs report available on request from Deloitte.
  • Mullvad VPN: listed in the company's menu of comparisons but not in the homepage table, so check mullvad.net for its terms.
  • Private Internet Access: named in the table's footnotes as another Kape brand; Kape is based in the UK.

When to stay

Stay if open source apps, published audits and a Swiss base are what you are choosing on, since those are the points the company documents in detail. The free plan is another reason: you can keep a VPN on one device at no cost while you compare. The review weighs those points with a score, and the free vs Plus page shows how far the free tier goes.

If you only need one device and do not care where you appear, you may not need to switch at all; try the free plan from the download page first.

How to compare fairly

  • Compare the renewal price, not only the first year offer.
  • Read each rival's own audit page, not a summary written by a competitor.
  • Check the device limit against the number of screens in your home.
  • Check whether the features you need are in the cheapest plan.
  • Use refund windows: this service gives 30 days on paid plans.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Proton VPN?

It depends on what you value. If open source apps and published audits matter most, the company's own table argues none of the big four rivals match it; if price or one feature matters more, compare each rival's current pricing page directly.

Is it better than NordVPN?

This site has not tested either service. The vendor's table says it has open source apps and a public no logs audit while NordVPN does not, and that NordVPN's Basic plan lacks an ad and malware blocker. Check NordVPN's own site for its side.

Who owns ExpressVPN and CyberGhost?

According to the table on the homepage, both are owned by Kape, which also owns Private Internet Access. The table's footnotes place Kape in the UK.

Is the comparison table neutral?

No. It is published by the company it favours and was last updated in April 2026. Use it as a list of questions to check, not as a verdict.

Can I keep the free plan while I try another VPN?

Yes. The free plan has no time limit, so you can keep it on one device while you test a rival, and paid plans have a 30 day money back guarantee.
