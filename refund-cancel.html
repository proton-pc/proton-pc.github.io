Proton VPN refund and cancellation: the 30 day guarantee explained

The Proton VPN refund policy is a 30 day money back guarantee on every paid subscription, and you can cancel at any time. This page explains what the company publishes, what it leaves to its terms, and how to avoid an unwanted renewal.

Proton VPN refunds paid subscriptions under a 30 day money back guarantee if you ask within the first 30 days, and you can cancel at any time. The pricing page points to the company's Terms and Conditions for the full refund rules. Plan changes get prorated credit.

What the guarantee covers

The Proton VPN refund promise is short. The pricing FAQ says you may cancel your subscription at any time and that the company gives a money back guarantee on all subscriptions when the refund is requested within the first 30 days. The homepage adds that it refunds the payment in full inside that window.

The same 30 day promise appears on each paid plan card and on the Windows and Apple TV download pages. It covers VPN Plus and the Unlimited bundle on any term, monthly or yearly.

What the plan pages leave to the terms

The plan pages do not say what happens to a refund request after day 30, how long the money takes to come back, or how purchases made through the App Store or Google Play are handled. Instead they link to the company's Terms and Conditions for the full refund policy.

This guide did not read those terms, so it does not quote them. If any of these points matter to you, read the terms on the official site before you buy, and keep the date of your purchase so you know when the 30 days end.

Avoiding an unwanted renewal

The larger cost risk is the renewal, not the refund. Paid plans renew automatically. On the current offer, VPN Plus moves from $35.88 in the first year to $83.88 a year, and the Unlimited bundle from $77.88 to $119.88. Note your renewal date when you subscribe and decide a few weeks before it whether to keep the plan.

The pricing page of this guide lists every term with its renewal price, so you can see the second year bill before you buy the first.

Switching or downgrading instead of cancelling

If the problem is the plan rather than the service, switching may suit you better than cancelling. The company lets you change term or plan at any time and applies prorated credit, for example when moving from one month of VPN Plus to a year, or from VPN Plus to the Unlimited bundle.

Moving down from Unlimited means fitting your account into the smaller plan, which can mean deactivating email addresses or calendars and cutting storage. Plan for that before you switch.

Cancelling and keeping the free plan

The free plan has no time limit, so leaving a paid plan does not have to mean losing the VPN. The plan pages do not describe exactly when paid features stop after you cancel, so check your account dashboard for the date.

If you decide the service is not for you, the alternatives page lists the rivals the company compares itself with. To come back later, the download page has the apps.

Frequently asked questions

Does Proton VPN give refunds?

Yes. All paid subscriptions have a 30 day money back guarantee if you request the refund within the first 30 days. The full rules are in the company's Terms and Conditions.

Can I cancel at any time?

Yes, the pricing FAQ says you can cancel at any time. To avoid paying the higher renewal price, cancel before your term renews.

Is the refund full or partial?

Within the 30 days the homepage says the payment is refunded in full. The plan pages do not describe refunds after day 30; the Terms and Conditions are the place to check.

What payment methods are accepted?

Visa, Mastercard, American Express, PayPal and Proton credits. Credits can be bought with cash, a bank transfer or Bitcoin after you create an account.

Will I lose the VPN if I cancel?

No. You can keep using the free plan, which has no time limit, on one device with servers in 10 countries chosen at random.
